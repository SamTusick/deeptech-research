01 / 05 Mission brief

A first-year team with a flight-hardware problem to solve.

Most engineering education stops at the analysis. We start there and keep going: tolerance stacks, layup schedules, ejection charge sizing, range safety paperwork, and the uncomfortable moment where a number on a screen becomes a part that either holds or doesn't.

DeepTech is FGCU's student rocketry and advanced-hardware team. We run as seven divisions on a single integrated program, so a freshman with no shop experience and a senior running structural analysis are working the same vehicle, to the same review gates, on the same calendar.

Season 01 has one deliverable: put Aquila-1 on the rail, recover it intact, and come back with data good enough to design the next one.

02 / 05 Program

Three vehicles. One line of increasing difficulty.

Each phase exists to earn the right to attempt the next one. Nothing gets skipped.

Phase 01 · Fall 2026

Aquila‑1

Single-stage solid. Commercial motor, student airframe, student avionics, dual-deploy recovery. The point is to fly clean and get the team through a full design–build–review–fly cycle.

Go for build
Phase 02 · Spring 2027

Aquila‑2

10,000 ft class. Custom flight computer, live telemetry downlink, filament-wound composite airframe, and an instrumented payload bay open to other FGCU labs.

In design
Phase 03 · 2027–28

Vertex

Propulsive descent testbed — thrust vector control, active guidance, and a landing attempt. Low-altitude hop campaign first; the flight article comes later.

03 / 05 Divisions

Seven divisions. Pick where you want to be wrong first.

No prior experience required for any of them. Hover or tap a division to read the brief.

Div 01 — Propulsion

Make the thrust curve real

Motor selection and characterization for AQ-1, static fire instrumentation, and the long-horizon work toward a student hybrid.

  • Thrust stand design & load-cell calibration
  • Motor performance modeling
  • Propellant handling & safety protocol
  • Hybrid feed-system research
Div 02 — Airframe & Structures

Everything that has to not break

Body tubes, fin cans, bulkheads, couplers and the load paths between them. CAD to cut parts, not to make pictures.

  • SolidWorks / Fusion modeling
  • Carbon & fiberglass layup
  • FEA on flight loads
  • Machining & additive fabrication
Div 03 — Avionics

The vehicle's nervous system

Flight computer hardware and firmware: sensing, logging, deployment events, and the radio link back to the ground station.

  • PCB design in KiCad
  • Embedded C / C++ on STM32
  • Barometric & IMU sensor fusion
  • LoRa telemetry & ground software
Div 04 — Recovery

Bring it back flyable

Dual-deployment architecture, parachute sizing, ejection charge testing, and the shock-cord math nobody thinks about until it fails.

  • Descent-rate & drift analysis
  • Black powder charge ground testing
  • Parachute fabrication
  • Deployment reliability review
Div 05 — Guidance & Control

The Vertex problem

Six-degree-of-freedom simulation now; thrust vector control and propulsive landing algorithms as the program matures.

  • 6-DOF flight simulation
  • Control-loop design & tuning
  • TVC gimbal prototyping
  • State estimation / Kalman filtering
Div 06 — Payload & Research

Give the flight a reason

Instrumented payloads flown for FGCU research groups and for our own aerodynamic and environmental data collection.

  • Sensor package integration
  • Data reduction & analysis
  • Environmental & vibration testing
  • Interface control documentation
Div 07 — Mission Operations

Nothing flies without this

Launch day runs on checklists, waivers and comms. Ops also owns the budget, the sponsor relationships and outreach.

  • Range safety & FAA waiver process
  • Launch checklists & comms protocol
  • Budget, procurement & sponsorship
  • Outreach, media & documentation

04 / 05 Flight 001

Aquila‑1

Named for the eagle and the constellation both. AQ-1 is deliberately unglamorous: a commercial motor, a conservative fin geometry, and every gram of ambition spent on the systems we actually have to learn — recovery, avionics and launch operations.
